Create a pollinator stop on your balcony

By Garden.City Editorial · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Bees, butterflies, hoverflies and moths move through cities by hopping between patches of flowers. Every planted balcony, rooftop and front step can be one of those stepping stones. A pollinator stop doesn’t need much room — a few well-chosen containers make a real difference — and it will improve harvests from your tomatoes, beans and strawberries too.

The four things pollinators need

  1. Flowers from spring to autumn — nectar and pollen across the whole season, not just one burst in June.
  2. The right flower shapes — open, flat flowers (daisies, sedum) feed short-tongued bees and hoverflies; tubular flowers (salvias, lavender) feed long-tongued bees and butterflies.
  3. Water — a shallow saucer with pebbles so insects can drink without drowning.
  4. No pesticides — including “natural” sprays that still harm beneficial insects.

Best container plants for pollinators

Spring

Crocus and grape hyacinth bulbs, flowering chives, early-blooming herbs like rosemary, and pansies or violas for early colour.

Summer

Lavender, thyme, oregano and marjoram allowed to flower, borage, zinnias, dwarf sunflowers, nasturtiums, cosmos and basil allowed to bloom at the end of the season.

Late summer to autumn

Sedums, asters, coneflowers, black-eyed Susans and late salvias keep food available when many gardens are finished.

Go native where you can. Plants native to your region support many more local insect species, including specialist bees. Ask a local native-plant nursery or extension office for a short list suited to containers.

Design tips for small spaces

  • Group several pots of the same plant together — insects find big blocks of colour more easily than single stems.
  • Give flowers the sunniest spot; most pollinator plants need at least six hours of sun.
  • Mix heights: a trailing nasturtium, a mid-height lavender and a dwarf sunflower in one corner.
  • Deadhead regularly to extend flowering, but leave some seedheads in autumn for birds.

Shelter for solitary bees

Most bee species are solitary and don’t sting aggressively. A small bee hotel made of clean, smooth-sided tubes or drilled hardwood, mounted facing the morning sun at chest height, can host mason and leafcutter bees. Choose designs with replaceable tubes and clean or replace them each year to prevent disease and parasites.
